Transparency and Oversight

Transparency is important when surveillance technology is used in a community. Residents may want to know where cameras are located, why they are being used, and what policies control their use.

Oversight can include rules explaining who may access collected information, when searches are permitted, and how the system is monitored for misuse.

Communities may also want opportunities to discuss surveillance technology before new systems are purchased or expanded. Public policies and clear documentation can help residents better understand how the technology is being used.

Questions About Oversight

  • Who approves the installation of cameras?
  • Are camera locations publicly available?
  • Are searches of the system documented?
  • Who investigates possible misuse?
  • Can residents review policies governing the system?

Transparency and Oversight in the News

Flock Safety: Updates to Privacy, Accountability, and Transparency

On August 13, 2026, Flock Safety published an article explaining changes the company is making to improve privacy, security, and accountability. The company announced a recommended seven-day data retention period for license plate reader systems, while allowing communities to choose their own retention policies.

Flock Safety also discussed new safeguards intended to help prevent misuse of its technology. These include monitoring for unusual search activity, requiring case codes for law enforcement searches, and temporarily restricting access when suspicious activity is detected.

The company also encourages law enforcement agencies to use public transparency portals. These portals can provide information about camera policies, data retention periods, data sharing, and search activity.

These changes represent Flock Safety's stated approach to oversight and privacy. Since the information comes directly from the company, it is also important to consider independent reporting when evaluating how effectively these safeguards work.
